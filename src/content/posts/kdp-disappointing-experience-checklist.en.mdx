Most independent authors assume KDP rejections only happen for copyright plagiarism or obvious AI spam. In reality, many accounts run into **"Disappointing Customer Experience"** blocks over subtle structural details that automated scanners and print-review thumbnail inspectors flag instantly.

## 1. Thin Content & Low-Content ISBN Misclassification

Applying for a free KDP ISBN on a 40-page pamphlet or a manuscript padded with boxy scorecard tables and blank fill-in prompts frequently triggers misleading-classification rejections.

- Keep serious non-fiction monographs **above 15,000+ words** of cohesive prose.
- Eliminate rectangular bordered card boxes and words like _Scorecard_, _Daily Ledger_, or _Printable Worksheet_ from print interiors unless publishing explicitly under Low Content rules.

## 2. Clinical DSM-5 & Miracle Cure Phrasing

Using medical diagnostic nouns (_panic attacks_, _insomnia_, _clinical anxiety_, _PTSD_, _ADHD_) alongside promises like _"60-second miracle"_ or _"cure in real time"_ violates marketplace health-claim policies. Reframe lifestyle guidance around grounded everyday habits: _sensory overwhelm_, _restless evenings_, and _mindful grounding_.

## 3. Broken eBook Hyperlinks (HTTP 429)

When KDP's headless crawler verifies external `<a href="http...">` links inside your `.epub`, storefront redirects or rate-limited landing pages frequently return `429 Too Many Requests`. Remove clickable external HTTP links inside the eBook interior and display plain-text companion URLs instead.
